Free Forever Fitness

Everyone has the right to be fit.

Not the right to afford it. The right to it. So here is a real training and food log that is free forever, works with the aeroplane mode on, and sends nothing anywhere. No account. No cookies. No trackers. Open source, under a licence that keeps it open.

Seven promises

Every claim here can be checked in a browser.

Anyone can write “we respect your privacy”. It costs nothing and proves nothing. So each promise below comes with the way to verify it yourself — no trust required, and none asked for.

  • 1 Nothing you log leaves your device

    Your sets, your food, your bodyweight, your photos. All of it is written to your browser’s own storage. There is no server holding a copy, because there is no server.

    Check it: open DevTools → Network, then log a workout. Every request is a GET fetching the app itself. There is no POST, because nothing is sent.

  • 2 No account, no email, no password

    Open it and start logging. There is no sign-up wall, no “continue with” button, and no profile. Nobody — including me — can look up your data, because nothing identifies you.

    Check it: there is no login screen to find. The app has no authentication code in it at all.

  • 3 Zero cookies

    Not “essential cookies only”. None. That is also why you will never see a consent banner here: there is nothing to consent to.

    Check it: type document.cookie in the console. It is an empty string.

  • 4 No trackers, no analytics, no third parties

    No Google Analytics, no Sentry, no ad SDK, no font CDN. The app talks to exactly one domain: its own.

    Check it: the Network tab shows every request going to freeforeverfitness.app and nowhere else.

  • 5 Works with the aeroplane mode on

    The whole app installs to your device, including a 913-exercise catalogue and a searchable food database. Basement gyms, aeroplanes, Indian Railways — it does not care.

    Check it: open it once, turn off your Wi-Fi, then search for an exercise. It answers instantly, from your device.

  • 6 Your data leaves whenever you want it to

    One tap in More → App downloads everything as plain JSON. Not a support ticket, not a 30-day wait, not a redacted subset.

    Check it: the exporter finds your data by scanning storage at runtime, so it cannot quietly miss a category somebody forgot to add to a list. If a key is unreadable it tells you which, rather than handing you a file that looks complete.

  • 7 Open source, under a licence that keeps it open

    The app is AGPL-3.0: anyone who runs a modified copy has to publish their changes. The reusable parts are Apache-2.0 so they can be reused freely. Contributions need no CLA and no copyright assignment — you keep your work.

    Check it: read the code, the decision records and the arguments behind them — the whole repository, including the parts that went wrong.

The rules

Five constraints, written down before the code.

These are not aspirations in a blog post. They are checked into the repository, they are enforced in the build, and features get rejected for breaking them.

  1. Free forever is a build constraint, not a promise. No feature may add a cost that grows with the number of people using it. If a feature needs a limit to be safe, the limit ships in the same change as the feature — never “later”. ADR-0001
  2. It has to work without AI at all. Every feature’s plain, deterministic version is built first and has to stand on its own. AI is a garnish here, never load-bearing — so the app cannot quietly become a subscription the day a model bill arrives. ADR-0016, ADR-0034
  3. Reads come from your device, never a query. Charts and search read what is already on your phone. A server round-trip to draw your own history is treated as a bug, not an optimisation — which is what makes offline the normal case rather than a special mode. ADR-0005, ADR-0006
  4. Accessibility is not negotiable. WCAG 2.2 AA. Works at 200% text size. Full keyboard operation. Colour is never the only signal. Hit targets are 48px, and 56px for anything you tap mid-set with one hand. The build fails if these regress. ADR-0013
  5. Anything worth arguing about gets written down. Thirty-four architecture decisions, each with the context, the alternatives and what it costs. Including the ones that turned out wrong — those are superseded in public, not deleted. ADR-0002

Refusals

What this will never have.

Promising features is easy. Refusing revenue is the part you can actually check — so here is the list, and it is not going to change.

  • ✕No ads. Not banners, not "sponsored" foods, not a supplement storefront.
  • ✕No public feed and no stranger DMs. This is a log, not a network. Nobody is ranked against you.
  • ✕No engagement-bait streaks. Missing a Tuesday is not a moral failure and will not be treated as one.
  • ✕No selling your data. There is no account, so there is nothing to sell and nobody to sell it to.
  • ✕No paywall appearing later. The architecture makes the running cost near zero. There will be nothing to start charging for.

Being straight with you

What isn't built yet.

A page like this usually implies everything is finished. This one is early, and pretending otherwise would undercut the rest of it.

  • No accounts and no sync — your log lives on one device. Take a backup.
  • Importers from MyFitnessPal, Strong and Hevy are not written yet.
  • Food coverage outside the US is thin. Custom foods and recipes fill the gap for now.
  • No native app. It installs from the browser and behaves like one.
  • The repository is going public shortly and is not open to read yet, so the source link above will not resolve until it is. The licences are already in force — AGPL-3.0 for the app, Apache-2.0 for the reusable packages — and none of that changes on the day it opens.
  • Two promises in the decision records are not kept yet: the monthly cloud bill is meant to be committed to the repository, and the build transcripts are meant to be published after a redaction pass. Both directories exist; both are still empty.

And the money

How this is possible at all.

Not charity, and not a runway waiting to run out. The industry charges for software whose actual running cost rounds to nothing.

Three things in fitness are genuinely expensive: paid humans, hosted video, and unmetered AI. None of them are what you open the app for. Everything else — the logging, the search, the charts — costs cents per user per month to run. So it is given away, and the expensive things are simply not built.
