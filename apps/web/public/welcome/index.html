Everyone has the right to be fit.

Not the right to afford it. The right to it. Strength, and knowing what you ate and what you lifted, should not depend on what you can pay each month.

Why this exists

Fitness is not a product. It is something you are owed.

A body is the one thing every person is given and has to live in. Looking after it — moving, eating, paying attention to both — is not a lifestyle purchase. It is maintenance on the only thing you cannot replace.

A pencil and a notebook did this for a century, and they worked. Then it became a monthly bill. Scanning a barcode: paid. Setting your own macro targets: paid. Seeing more than a fortnight of your own history: paid. Your own effort, metered back to you.

Somewhere there is a sixteen-year-old with a second-hand barbell, and someone training in a room with no gym for forty kilometres, and someone whose budget has no line for a subscription. None of them should get a worse tool than anyone else. That is the whole reason this exists, and the rest of this page is how it is actually possible.

The principles

Free forever is a build constraint, not a promise.

Anyone can promise to stay free. The promise is worth nothing — the company changes hands, the runway ends, the pricing page quietly grows a tier. So this is enforced in the code instead, where you can check it.

No feature may cost per user

Anything whose bill grows with the number of people using it does not ship. The limit is written in the same change as the feature.

Your phone does the work

Search, charts and history are computed on your device. More people using it costs nothing to run, so there is nothing to recover from you.

It works with no signal

Gym basements, aeroplanes, dead zones. The catalogue is already downloaded.

Your data is yours to take

One button, one file, everything in it. Leaving is a supported action, not a support ticket.

Refusals

What this will never have.

Promising features is easy. Refusing revenue is the part you can actually check — so here is the list, and it is not going to change.

  • ✕No ads. Not banners, not "sponsored" foods, not a supplement storefront.
  • ✕No public feed and no stranger DMs. This is a log, not a network. Nobody is ranked against you.
  • ✕No engagement-bait streaks. Missing a Tuesday is not a moral failure and will not be treated as one.
  • ✕No selling your data. There is no account, so there is nothing to sell and nobody to sell it to.
  • ✕No paywall appearing later. The architecture makes the running cost near zero. There will be nothing to start charging for.

Being straight with you

What isn't built yet.

A page like this usually implies everything is finished. This one is early, and pretending otherwise would undercut the rest of it.

  • No accounts and no sync — your log lives on one device. Take a backup.
  • Importers from MyFitnessPal, Strong and Hevy are not written yet.
  • Food coverage outside the US is thin. Custom foods and recipes fill the gap for now.
  • No native app. It installs from the browser and behaves like one.

And the money

How this is possible at all.

Not charity, and not a runway waiting to run out. The industry charges for software whose actual running cost rounds to nothing.

Three things in fitness are genuinely expensive: paid humans, hosted video, and unmetered AI. None of them are what you open the app for. Everything else — the logging, the search, the charts — costs cents per user per month to run. So it is given away, and the expensive things are simply not built.
